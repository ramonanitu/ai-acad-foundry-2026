After a complaint is closed (step 8 of
[complaints-intake-procedure.md](complaints-intake-procedure.md)), Libra Bank sends
a short satisfaction survey within 1 business day, by app notification or email,
whichever channel the complaint was originally logged through. The survey asks
three questions: were you kept informed, was the outcome explained clearly, and
are you satisfied with the outcome.

**If you are not satisfied**, the survey itself offers two paths, and it is
important to pick the right one:

- **Reopen this complaint.** Use this when you believe the original investigation
  missed something, misunderstood your case, or reached the wrong conclusion on the
  same facts. Reopening keeps the same tracking number and the same evidence
  already on file, and restarts the resolution clock from the category's target in
  [complaints-sla-table.md](complaints-sla-table.md). A complaint can be reopened
  this way within **10 business days** of the closing letter; after that window,
  file a new complaint instead, referencing the old tracking number.
- **Escalate.** Use this when you accept the facts as investigated but disagree
  with the outcome or want an independent review. This takes you to
  [complaints-escalation-procedure.md](complaints-escalation-procedure.md) —
  supervisor review, then Head of Complaints, then CSALB.

A complaint can only be reopened once under the same tracking number; a second
disagreement after a reopened investigation goes to escalation, not a second
reopening, so that a case cannot cycle indefinitely inside the same team.

Survey responses (not just for dissatisfied customers) feed into the quarterly
reporting described in
[complaints-record-keeping.md](complaints-record-keeping.md) and are reviewed by
category owners to spot recurring problems, independent of any individual case
outcome.
