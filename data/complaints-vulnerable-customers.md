title: Complaint handling for vulnerable customers

Libra Bank applies additional flexibility, not a different process, when a
complainant is identified as vulnerable. The intake, categorization, and
escalation steps in [complaints-intake-procedure.md](complaints-intake-procedure.md)
still apply; what changes is timing, communication, and who gets involved.

**Who this applies to:** customers experiencing, temporarily or permanently, any
of: significant illness or disability affecting their ability to engage with the
standard process, bereavement, financial hardship or over-indebtedness, limited
digital literacy or language ability, or advanced age combined with any of the
above. A customer does not need to self-declare a formal diagnosis — a staff
member's reasonable judgment that extra care is warranted is enough to flag a case.

**What changes:**

- A **dedicated contact person** is assigned for the life of the complaint, so the
  customer is not asked to re-explain the issue to a new person each time they
  call.
- Resolution targets from [complaints-sla-table.md](complaints-sla-table.md) may be
  **paused, not just extended**, if the customer needs more time to provide
  information (for example, gathering documents with help from a family member or
  support worker) — the clock resumes when the customer confirms they are ready to
  continue.
- Written communication is offered in plain-language format on request, and a
  phone call to talk through a decision letter is offered proactively rather than
  waiting for the customer to ask.
- Goodwill assessment under
  [complaints-eligibility-goodwill.md](complaints-eligibility-goodwill.md) may
  account for the disproportionate impact an error had given the customer's
  circumstances, at the case owner's discretion within the amounts in
  [complaints-goodwill-amounts.md](complaints-goodwill-amounts.md) — the amount
  bands do not change, but where a case falls within a band can.

Flagging a customer as vulnerable is never used to close a complaint faster or with
less scrutiny than a standard case; the intent is exclusively to remove friction for
the customer, not to change the outcome the bank would otherwise reach.
