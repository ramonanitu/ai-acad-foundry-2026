A **goodwill payment** is money Libra Bank pays a customer when a complaint is
upheld, separate from correcting the underlying error itself (a wrongly-charged fee
is always reversed regardless of goodwill; goodwill is the extra compensation for
the inconvenience or loss the error caused).

You are eligible for a goodwill payment when **all** of the following are true:

1. The complaint was upheld or partially upheld — a rejected complaint is never
   eligible, even if you disagree with the decision.
2. The root cause was a proven Libra Bank error or failure: a processing mistake, a
   system outage, a missed deadline, or incorrect information given by staff.
   Errors caused by the customer (e.g., entering a wrong IBAN) are not eligible,
   even if Libra Bank later helps recover the funds.
3. The complaint was filed within 6 months of the customer becoming aware of the
   issue. Complaints filed later are still investigated but are not eligible for
   goodwill, only for correction of the underlying error.
4. The matter is not already the subject of litigation or a CSALB/ANPC case in
   progress — those follow their own compensation rules instead.

Two sub-cases affect the amount, covered in
[complaints-goodwill-amounts.md](complaints-goodwill-amounts.md):

- **Administrative inconvenience**, where the error caused delay, stress, or extra
  effort but no measurable financial loss (e.g., a card replacement took three
  weeks longer than promised).
- **Proven financial loss**, where the customer can document a direct monetary loss
  caused by the error (e.g., an overdraft interest charge that would not have
  occurred if a payment had processed on time).

Eligibility is assessed by the team that owns the complaint category (see
[complaints-taxonomy.md](complaints-taxonomy.md)), not by the customer's own
estimate of harm, and the decision is explained in the closing letter described in
[complaints-intake-procedure.md](complaints-intake-procedure.md).
