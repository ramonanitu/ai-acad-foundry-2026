---
title: Waiting period before CSALB escalation — 2026 rule
product: complaints
audience: retail
effective: 2026-01-15
version: 2
status: current
---

As of **15 January 2026**, a customer may refer an unresolved complaint to CSALB
(the Financial Consumer Ombudsman) once **15 business days** have passed from the
date the complaint was logged with Libra Bank, without a final written decision
having been issued. This is the figure to use for any complaint logged from
15 January 2026 onward.

This is a substantial reduction from the previous **45 calendar days** rule that
applied through 2025 (see [complaints-ombudsman-2025.md](complaints-ombudsman-2025.md)),
made possible by the shorter, category-specific resolution targets introduced in
the 2026 complaints policy (see
[complaints-sla-table.md](complaints-sla-table.md) and
[complaints-policy-2026.md](complaints-policy-2026.md)). The change was driven by a
National Bank of Romania directive requiring faster paths to alternative dispute
resolution for retail banking customers.

Note the units also changed, not just the number: the 2025 rule counted **calendar**
days, the 2026 rule counts **business** days — the two are not directly comparable
by number alone. Fifteen business days is roughly three working weeks, whereas
forty-five calendar days was roughly six and a half weeks; the practical wait for a
customer has been cut by more than half.

Complaints logged before 15 January 2026 that were still open on that date continue
under the 45-calendar-day rule they started with, consistent with the
non-retroactivity principle in
[complaints-policy-2026.md](complaints-policy-2026.md).
