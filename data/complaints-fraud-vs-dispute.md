Libra Bank treats fraud and ordinary card disputes very differently, and using the
wrong one slows your case down. This document explains how to tell them apart.

**It is a dispute (see [complaints-card-disputes-procedure.md](complaints-card-disputes-procedure.md))
when:**
- You recognize the merchant and made the purchase yourself, but the amount,
  delivery, or a subscription is wrong.
- You made the transaction but the merchant has not delivered, or you already
  cancelled a recurring charge.
- Your card and PIN were never out of your control.

**It is fraud when:**
- You do not recognize the merchant or the transaction at all.
- Your card was lost, stolen, or you suspect its details were copied (skimming,
  a fake terminal, a phishing message asking for your card number or one-time
  code).
- Someone accessed your online banking or app without your permission.
- You gave your card details or a one-time code to someone impersonating Libra
  Bank staff, the police, or a courier — this is still fraud, even though you
  technically "authorized" the transaction under pressure or deception.

**If it is fraud:**
1. Block the card immediately in the app (Cards → Block) or call 0800 123 400,
   available 24/7 for fraud specifically (unlike the general call center hours in
   [complaints-channels.md](complaints-channels.md)).
2. File a police report if funds were lost — Libra Bank's Fraud & Security team can
   guide you but cannot file it for you.
3. The Fraud & Security team investigates and reaches a provisional decision within
   5 business days, per [complaints-sla-table.md](complaints-sla-table.md) — faster
   than any dispute category, and with no extension path.
4. If fraud is confirmed and you were not grossly negligent (e.g., you did not
   write your PIN on the card), funds are reimbursed in full, independent of any
   goodwill payment under [complaints-goodwill-amounts.md](complaints-goodwill-amounts.md).

If you are not sure which applies, file through any channel and say so — the
receiving team routes ambiguous cases to Fraud & Security first, since misrouting a
real fraud case as an ordinary dispute is the costlier mistake.
